How I work

The judgement behind the cases

Four questions I am usually asked, answered with the decisions they came from.

How do you decide what not to build?

Three questions, in order. Is it worth it, both for the business outcome and for whether the technical framework transfers to other scenarios later? Is it more than the situation actually needs? And if the direction is right, is now the moment?

On the event platform I chose not to build a priority and collision engine. The more complex the rules, the less a client could predict whether their campaign would run. Operations separated campaigns by time slot and area instead: manual, but predictable.

How do you work with engineers when the technology is not ready?

I work from both directions. From the business side: what is the pain, and can this technology touch it? From the technology side: what is it genuinely good at, what are its failure modes, and what is on its roadmap?

Then I ask: can I bound the scenario tightly enough, and design product responses carefully enough, that the remaining risk is acceptable? For complex junctions that meant mapping model confidence onto three strengths of guidance, so an uncertain detection stayed vague rather than wrong. Small trial, measure, then scale.

How do you handle disagreement across teams?

First I work out which kind it is, because the order matters. A disagreement about goals cannot be solved with data alone: both sides are usually right about their own objective. A disagreement about facts is an information gap, and it closes by sharing openly rather than arguing. Only when goals and facts are aligned is it useful to compare approaches, weighing benefit and cost together.

Underneath that: shared documents, meeting notes, a maintained dependency list, and asking for leadership support when a dependency will not move.

How do you use AI in your own work?

Like running a small team. One tool is strong at code, data and structured output; another at proposing solution paths; another at research and reading through literature. I match the task to the tool, split the work into clear pieces, and set what a good output looks like before I ask.

The part that matters most is having my own framework first. Without it you end up evaluating the model’s structure instead of the problem. I cross-check between tools and verify sources. On my dissertation the research synthesis and the analysis ran through different tools, each checking the other.
